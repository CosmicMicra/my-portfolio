a short story ↓

two things I love

I love AI, and I love security. For a long time I thought of them as two separate things, and in a lot of ways they are. They even use the same words, like token, model and agent, but those words mean completely different things in each field.

the part I like most

What I like most is where they meet. Using AI to help keep systems safe, and also making sure AI itself is safe to use. That second part matters more now that AI tools install things and take actions on their own, often without anyone checking.

before

Before my current job, I worked on cloud security. A lot of it was monitoring systems, measuring things and trying to understand why something looked off. I still enjoy that part, slowly figuring out how things actually work.

for kids

At Santa Clara's Frugal Innovation Hub, I worked on AI that helps children learn better. I trained and benchmarked models, and tried to keep safety in mind from the start, because the people using it were kids.

now

Right now I work at Rapticore, a cybersecurity company, on something called Orewatch. When someone, or some AI tool, is about to install a package, Orewatch looks inside it first and checks what it really does. If it's doing something it shouldn't, like trying to take your passwords, it stops it and explains why.

a question I keep coming back to

I'm also working on something called MCP drift. It started with a question I kept coming back to. Say you install a tool and it's safe. How do you know the person who made it didn't change it later? Most of the time, nobody checks. So I'm trying to find out how often that really happens.
