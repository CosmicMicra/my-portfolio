AI is learning to act on its own. Somebody has to check what it trusts.

a made-up story, about a very real kind of problem

Tuesday, 2:14 am.

Everyone's asleep. An AI coding assistant is still working, and it decides it needs a small library to format dates.

It finds one.

Nice name, thousands of downloads, a friendly README. Nothing about it looks wrong, so it installs it.

But nobody looked inside.

Go on, you look.

There it is.

A few quiet lines that pack up your secret keys and send them to a stranger. No alarms. Just one line, doing exactly what it was written to do.

so, what do I do?

    
I build the thing that looks inside.

    

      
Right now I work at Rapticore on a project called Overwatch. Before a package lands on your computer, or your AI assistant's, Overwatch opens it up and reads what it actually does. Is it reaching for your passwords? Talking to a server it has no reason to talk to?

      
If something's off, it stops the package and tells you why, in plain words, so a person can decide.

      
I love this space because AI is starting to do things instead of just answering questions, and every new thing it can do is something it has to be careful with.
